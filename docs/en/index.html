Nossa Senhora Aparecida — Chronology

Chronology of Nossa Senhora Aparecida — the image netted from the Paraíba do Sul in 1717, the devotion that grew around it, and the Church and state acts that honoured it, from the first chapel to the tercentenary

An open, source-referenced chronology of Nossa Senhora Aparecida: the terracotta image of Our Lady of the Conception reportedly found by three fishermen in the Paraíba do Sul river in 1717, the devotion and national shrine that grew around it, and the coronations, titles, patronage decrees, golden roses and papal visits that honoured it.

About

Work-in-progress dataset compiled from public sources (see references). There is no apparition in this case and no discernment judgment anywhere in it: what was found in 1717 was a statue, the finding is recorded here as a REPORTED event, and every subsequent Church act — coronation, basilica titles, the patronage decree, golden roses, consecrations — honours or regulates a devotion rather than adjudicating an event; honours are not findings, and this dataset never adds them up into one. The dating is carried honestly: the earliest surviving continuous account of the finding was written in August 1757, some forty years after the event, and opens 'No ano de 1719, pouco mais ou menos' ('in the year 1719, more or less'); the year 1717 is a historians' inference from the documented October 1717 passage of the Count of Assumar through Guaratinguetá, and no source attests a day — 12 October is a twentieth-century liturgical and civil convention. Several items — the 1901 petition details, the 1903 coronation grant, the 1967 golden-rose logistics, the foundation-stone story — rest on the Santuário's own media operation (A12) alone; A12 material is labeled official-devotional wherever cited. The text of the 16 July 1930 patronage decree is held offline in the Aparecida curia archive and no online primary text has been located. The CNBB's National Shrine declaration is dated 1984 by most secondaries and 1983 by the shrine's own page, and the dataset records the disagreement instead of resolving it. Contested size claims ('largest Marian shrine in the world') are attributed, never asserted. Single-source dates are flagged with a ? mark. Corrections against primary sources are welcome.

What it is
A Brazilian Marian devotion centred on a small terracotta image of Our Lady of the Conception, reportedly netted from the Paraíba do Sul river at Guaratinguetá (São Paulo) in 1717. 'Aparecida' — 'she who appeared' — names the image, not a vision: nothing in this case was ever reported as an apparition. The image has been principal Patroness of Brazil since 1930 and is kept at the national shrine in the city that grew around it.[8] [1] [13]
The finding (reported)
According to the account the Church treats as official, three fishermen of Guaratinguetá — Domingos Martins Garcia, João Alves and Filipe Pedroso — were ordered to supply fish for the passage of Dom Pedro de Almeida, Count of Assumar, en route to Minas Gerais. After a fishless stretch of the river, João Alves's drag-net brought up the body of an image of Our Lady of the Conception without the head; a later cast brought up the head, and abundant fishing followed. The episode is recorded here as reported tradition, first written down forty years later.[8] [1] [10]
Earliest attestation
The earliest surviving continuous account is the 'Notícia da Aparição da Imagem da Senhora', written in August 1757 by the vicar Pe. João de Morais e Aguiar in the Primeiro Livro do Tombo of the parish of Santo Antônio de Guaratinguetá — roughly forty years after the event. Earlier layers are reported (a 1743 report by the vicar José Alves Vilela; a report 'eight years after the event' per the Diocese of Piracicaba) but the attribution is tangled in the secondary literature and no earlier text has been verified here.[9] [10] [11]
The dating
The 1757 account itself opens 'No ano de 1719, pouco mais ou menos' — 'in the year 1719, more or less'. The year 1717 is a historians' correction, fixed by the shrine's historian Pe. Júlio Brustoloni CSsR from the documented passage of the Count of Assumar through Guaratinguetá in October 1717. No day is attested in any source: the shrine says 'second fortnight of October', Souza (citing Pasin) says 17 October, and the feast date of 12 October is a twentieth-century convention.[10] [9] [8]
The image
Terracotta, about 36 cm tall and 2.55 kg, originally polychrome ('barro claro', with a dark-blue mantle per Brustoloni); its dark colour is attributed to years in river mud and to lamp smoke. Attribution studies point to a Paulista workshop; tradition names the Benedictine sculptor Frei Agostinho de Jesus, an attribution that lacks consensus.[9] [13]
The honours
Canonical coronation (8 September 1904); the title of minor basilica for the old church (1908, the first in Brazil); principal Patroness of Brazil by decree of Pius XI (16 July 1930); three papal golden roses (1967, 2007, 2017); consecration of the new basilica by John Paul II (4 July 1980). Every one of these acts honours or regulates the devotion — an image, a church, a nation's patronage — and none of them adjudicates any event.[2] [8] [18] [20]
Custody
Redemptorist missionaries (CSsR), brought from Bavaria at the request of the Bishop of São Paulo, took charge of the shrine on 28 October 1894; their custody continues today.[6] [8]
The see
The Archdiocese of Aparecida was erected on 19 April 1958 by Pius XII (apostolic constitution Sacrorum antistitum) — directly as an archdiocese, not as a diocese later elevated. It was governed by apostolic administrators until the first resident archbishop, Cardinal Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta, was appointed in 1964.[17] [15]
The new basilica
Designed by Benedito Calixto de Jesus Neto; effective construction from 11 November 1955; a Greek cross of 188 × 183 m with a 70 m dome and a 109 m tower, about 23,000 m² of interior and a capacity of 30,000. Consecrated by John Paul II on 4 July 1980 and made cathedral of the archdiocese in November 2016. The Santuário describes itself as 'the largest shrine in the world dedicated to Mary' and Wikipédia-pt calls it the second-largest church in the world after St Peter's — both are attributed, contested metrics, never asserted here.[14] [3]
The 1978 breakage
On 16 May 1978 the image was seized during evening devotions and shattered into some 200 fragments, about 165 of them from the body. It was restored at the Museu de Arte de São Paulo (MASP) by Maria Helena Chartuni between 28 June and 31 July 1978 and returned to Aparecida.[12] [13]

José Alves Vilela, is reported to have informed episcopal authority of the devotion and the graces attributed to the image, in connection with authorizing the cult and building a proper chapel. This dataset records August 1757 as the earliest verified attestation and this 1743 report as reported but unpinned; checking Brustoloni (1979) directly — everyone's ultimate source — is the open task.
1745 1745-07-26 Morro dos Coqueiros, Aparecida, Brazil The first chapel is blessed on the Morro dos Coqueiros — The chapel built by the vicar José Alves Vilela on the Morro dos Coqueiros, on land donated by Margarida Nunes Rangel, is blessed and the image is transferred to it. This is the first public church of the devotion; the town of Aparecida later grows around the site.[1] [8] [13]

It opens 'No ano de 1719, pouco mais ou menos', and it is the first surviving document to name the three fishermen. Everything earlier is reported through later tellings; everything later depends on this text.[9] [8] [10]

Aparecida, Brazil Construction of a larger church — the future Basílica Velha — begins — Work begins on a larger church on the Morro dos Coqueiros to replace the 1745 chapel, as the pilgrimage outgrows it. Aparecida, Brazil Princess Isabel's first visit — Princess Isabel, heiress to the Brazilian throne, visits the shrine for the first time; the devotional tradition associates the visit with her promise of a crown for the image. 1880s
1888 1888-11-06 Aparecida, Brazil Princess Isabel donates the gold crown and the blue mantle — On her second visit, Princess Isabel donates a crown of gold and gems and a blue mantle for the image — the crown used in the canonical coronation of 1904 and the iconography carried ever since. The donation comes months after the Lei Áurea and months before the fall of the monarchy.[13] [4]
1888 1888-12-08 ? Aparecida, Brazil The new church — the Basílica Velha — is solemnly inaugurated — The larger church begun in 1834 is solemnly inaugurated on the feast of the Immaculate Conception, per Wikipédia-pt. It serves as the devotion's principal church until the new basilica of the twentieth century.[13]Single-source (Wikipédia-pt).
1890s
1894 1894-10-28 Aparecida, Brazil Redemptorist missionaries take charge of the shrine — Redemptorists of the Bavarian province, brought to Brazil at the request of the Bishop of São Paulo and led by Pe. Gebardo Wiggermann, arrive at Aparecida and assume custody of the shrine. Their custody — pastoral care, publishing, and later a radio, television and web operation — continues today.[6] [8]
1900s
1901 1901 Rio de Janeiro, Brazil The Brazilian episcopate petitions for a canonical coronation — The Brazilian episcopate, in a petition the Santuário's account says was signed by Archbishop Joaquim Arcoverde of Rio de Janeiro, requests from the Holy See the canonical coronation of the image. A coronation is a liturgical honour for a venerated image; the petition is the start of the honour's paper trail, not of any inquiry.[4]Year per the Santuário's coronation retrospective; the petition's details — including the signature of Archbishop Joaquim Arcoverde of Rio de Janeiro — rest on that account alone and are labeled official-devotional.
1903 1903-12-21 ? Rome The coronation is granted (per the Santuário's account, by the Chapter of St Peter's) — Under Pius X, the canonical coronation of the image is granted — per the Santuário's account, by decree of the Chapter of St Peter's Basilica dated 21 December 1903, received at Aparecida on 4 February 1904. The act authorizes an honour for an image; it makes no finding about the finding.[4]Single-source (A12). That the grant came from the Chapter of St Peter's Basilica — the body historically competent for canonical coronations — rather than from a papal decree proper is period-typical but rests on the Santuário's account alone here; some accounts say 'Pius X signed the decree'. The grant is said to have reached Aparecida on 4 February 1904.
1904 1904-09-08 Aparecida, Brazil Canonical coronation of the image — Dom José de Camargo Barros, Bishop of São Paulo, crowns the image with Princess Isabel's 1888 crown, before a crowd the accounts put at some 15,000 faithful and twelve to fourteen bishops (the counts vary). Devotional secondaries assert the presence of President Rodrigues Alves, which the Santuário's main account does not mention, and accounts differ over who presided (the nuncio) versus who crowned (Camargo Barros); the dataset records the differences. A canonical coronation honours a venerated image — it presupposes the devotion and adjudicates nothing.[4] [2]
1908 1908-04-29 Aparecida, Brazil Pius X grants the old church the title of minor basilica — the first in Brazil — The church of Aparecida receives the title of Basílica Menor, the first in Brazil, with the solemn proclamation on site on 5 September 1909. A basilica title is an honour for a building and its worship; like every act in this chronology, it is about an object — here, a church — and not about an event.[2] [13]
1930s
1930 1930-07-16 Rome Pius XI declares Our Lady Aparecida principal Patroness of Brazil — By papal decree of 16 July 1930, at the request of Cardinal Sebastião Leme — who had floated the title at the September 1929 Marian Congress, per the Santuário's account — Pius XI declares Nossa Senhora da Conceição Aparecida principal Patroness of Brazil, with all liturgical privileges. A patronage decree regulates a nation's devotion; it presupposes the cult and makes no finding about 1717.[8] [7] [2]The date and content are consistent across academic and official sources, but the decree itself is held offline in the Aparecida curia archive ('papéis avulsos', per Pasin as cited by Souza) and no online primary text has been located; it should be checkable in Acta Apostolicae Sedis 22 (1930) — a library task still open.
1931 1931-05-31 Rio de Janeiro, Brazil Brazil is solemnly consecrated to its Patroness in Rio de Janeiro — The image is taken to Rio de Janeiro by special train for the solemn proclamation of the patronage; Cardinal Leme presides before a crowd put at about one million faithful, 'diante do governo e seus ministros' — before the government and its ministers — per Souza. The ceremony is the civic-religious high-water mark of the patronage, and, like the decree it celebrates, an act about a devotion.[2] [8]
1940s
1946 1946-09-10 Aparecida, Brazil Foundation stone of the new basilica — The foundation stone of a vastly larger new basilica is laid. The Santuário's own history records that the stone was stolen that same night and re-laid in August 1954 — an anecdote resting on the shrine's telling alone.[3]
1950s
1955 1955-11-11 Aparecida, Brazil Effective construction of the new basilica begins — Construction of the new basilica begins in earnest to the design of Benedito Calixto de Jesus Neto: a Greek cross of 188 × 183 m, a 70 m dome, a 109 m tower, some 23,000 m² of interior and a capacity of 30,000. The building will take decades; pilgrims begin using the unfinished nave in 1959.[3] [14]
1958 1958-04-19 Aparecida, Brazil Pius XII erects the Archdiocese of Aparecida — directly as an archdiocese — By the apostolic constitution Sacrorum antistitum, Pius XII erects the Archdiocese of Aparecida — directly as a metropolitan archdiocese, not as a diocese later elevated. The see is governed by apostolic administrators until 1964. The erection of a see around a shrine is an act of ecclesiastical organization; it honours the devotion's weight and judges nothing about its origin.[17] [15]
1960s
1964 1964-04-18 Aparecida, Brazil First resident archbishop: Cardinal Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta — Cardinal Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta is appointed the first resident Archbishop of Aparecida, ending the apostolic administration that had run since the see's erection in 1958. Some accounts misread 1964 as the see's elevation to archdiocese; it is the first archbishop's appointment — the see was an archdiocese from 1958.[17]
1967 1967-08-15 ? Aparecida, Brazil Golden rose no. 1 — Paul VI, for the 250th jubilee of the finding — Paul VI grants the shrine its first golden rose for the 250th anniversary of the finding, delivered — per the Santuário's account — on 15 August 1967. The golden rose is a papal honour bestowed on shrines, images and persons; it decorates a devotion and certifies nothing about its origin.[5] [18] [16]The grant of the rose for the 250th jubilee is corroborated (CNBB, Wikipédia-pt); the delivery date of 15 August 1967 and the identification of the legate rest on the Santuário's account alone and are flagged.
1970s
1978 1978-05-16 Aparecida, Brazil The image is seized and shattered — During evening devotions, Rogério Marcos de Oliveira, aged 19, seizes the image from its niche; in the struggle it falls and shatters into some 200 fragments, about 165 of them from the body. Characterizations of the perpetrator — 'Protestant iconoclast', accounts of his motives and later fate — vary across sources and are theirs, not this dataset's.[12] [13]
1978 1978-07-31 São Paulo, Brazil The restoration at MASP is completed — Maria Helena Chartuni, head of the restoration department of the Museu de Arte de São Paulo, completes the reassembly and restoration of the shattered image — a jigsaw of some 200 fragments — begun on 28 June under director Pietro Maria Bardi. The image returns to Aparecida in an open fire-brigade car through crowds along the route.[12]The restoration dates (28 June – 31 July 1978) are well-sourced; the date of the image's return to Aparecida appears as both 'mid-August' and 19 August in different tellings and is left unpinned.
1980s
1980 1980-06-30 Brasília, Brazil Law 6.802/1980 makes 12 October a national holiday in honour of the Patroness — Brazil's Congress enacts Law 6.802 of 30 June 1980, declaring 12 October a national holiday in honour of Nossa Senhora Aparecida, Patroness of Brazil. The holiday is the civil act that fixed 12 October in the national calendar — a date no colonial source attests for the finding itself.[8]
1980 1980-07-04 Aparecida, Brazil John Paul II consecrates the new basilica — On the first papal visit to the shrine, John Paul II consecrates the new basilica — 'I come, therefore, to consecrate this Basilica', his homily says — and the building receives the title of minor basilica. The homily retells the finding of 1717 and recalls the 1978 breakage and restoration. A consecration dedicates a building for worship; the pope's retelling of the tradition is a devotional act, not a historical finding.[20] [14]
1984 1983 or 1984 ? Aparecida, Brazil The CNBB declares the basilica the National Shrine (1983/1984 — act and date unpinned) — The National Conference of Bishops of Brazil declares the new basilica the National Shrine of Nossa Senhora Aparecida. The declaration is well-attested in secondary accounts but the primary act has not been located, and the year itself is disputed between the shrine's own page (1983) and most other accounts (1984).[14] [3]Wikipédia-pt and most secondaries date the CNBB declaration 1984; the Santuário's own construction-history page says 1983; no primary CNBB act has been located online. Both candidates are recorded and the discrepancy is left unresolved.
2000s
2007 2007-05-13 Aparecida, Brazil Benedict XVI at Aparecida: golden rose no. 2 and the opening of CELAM's Fifth General Conference — Benedict XVI visits the shrine, presents its second golden rose (12 May, per Wikipédia-pt), and on 13 May celebrates Mass inaugurating the Fifth General Conference of the Latin American and Caribbean bishops (CELAM), which meets at the shrine until 31 May and produces the 'Aparecida Document'. The document is the conference's conclusions on evangelization, drafted at the shrine — not an act about the image.[21] [16] [14]The Mass and the inauguration of the CELAM conference on 13 May are vatican.va's own record; the presentation of the golden rose on 12 May rests on Wikipédia-pt.
2010s
2013 2013-07-24 Aparecida, Brazil Francis celebrates Mass at the shrine during World Youth Day Rio — In his first international journey, Francis comes to Aparecida during World Youth Day Rio, celebrates Mass in the basilica, recalls the fishermen of the tradition and the 2007 conference at which he had led the drafting committee, and entrusts his pontificate to Our Lady of Aparecida.[22]
2016 2016-11 ? Aparecida, Brazil The new basilica becomes the cathedral of the archdiocese — The national shrine's basilica is made the cathedral of the Archdiocese of Aparecida, uniting the metropolitan see and the national shrine in one church.[14]Month per Wikipédia-pt; single-source, day not established.
2017 2017-10-09 Aparecida, Brazil Golden rose no. 3 — sent by Francis for the tercentenary — A golden rose sent by Francis for the tercentenary of the finding is delivered by the cardinal legate Giovanni Battista Re to Archbishop Orlando Brandes. It is the shrine's third — 1967, 2007, 2017 — and was exceptional in its year, Fátima having received 2017's rose on 13 May; like the others, it honours a shrine and adjudicates nothing.[18] [19]
2017 2017-10-12 Aparecida, Brazil The tercentenary: Francis's video message to Brazil — On the conventional feast, the tercentenary of the finding is celebrated at the National Shrine, with Cardinal Giovanni Battista Re presiding as pontifical delegate and a video message from Francis to the Brazilian people — 'confiando-lhe o meu pontificado', entrusting his pontificate to the Patroness once more. Wikipédia-pt puts the jubilee year's visitors at about 13 million.[23] [14]

Key figures

Domingos Martins Garcia, João Alves and Filipe Pedroso

Brazil

The three fishermen of Guaratinguetá to whom the reported finding of 1717 is attributed — João Alves credited with netting the image, Filipe Pedroso's family its first custodians for some fifteen years. Attestation caveat: they are named only in the 1757 Livro do Tombo account, written forty years after the event; Brustoloni (1979) verified their existence in Guaratinguetá baptism and marriage registers, per Lopes. Their existence is documented; the fishing episode is the reported tradition[9] [8] [1]

Pe. João de Morais e Aguiar

Brazil

Vicar of Guaratinguetá; author of the 'Notícia da Aparição da Imagem da Senhora' (August 1757) in the parish's Primeiro Livro do Tombo — the earliest surviving continuous account of the finding, and the text every later telling depends on[9] [8]

Pe. José Alves Vilela

Brazil

Vicar of Guaratinguetá; builder of the first chapel, blessed 26 July 1745; associated in parts of the secondary literature with a 1743 report to episcopal authority on the cult — an attribution tangled with the 1757 text and not resolved here[1] [10] [11]

Frei Agostinho de Jesus

Brazil

Benedictine sculptor of colonial São Paulo to whom tradition attributes the image. Attribution studies (Pedro de Oliveira Ribeiro Netto, 1967/1970; Brustoloni, 1979) point to a Paulista workshop; the attribution to Frei Agostinho specifically lacks consensus, and the dataset records it as tradition[13] [9]

Princess Isabel

1846–1921 · Brazil

Heiress to the Brazilian throne; visited the shrine in 1868 and again on 6 November 1888, donating the gold-and-gems crown and blue mantle used in the 1904 canonical coronation[13] [4]

Cardinal Sebastião Leme

1882–1942 · Brazil

Archbishop of Rio de Janeiro; proposed the patroness title at the September 1929 Marian Congress (per the Santuário's account), obtained the decree of Pius XI of 16 July 1930, and presided over the solemn consecration of Brazil to the Patroness in Rio on 31 May 1931[8] [2] [7]

Cardinal Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta

Brazil

First resident Archbishop of Aparecida, appointed 18 April 1964 after the see — erected directly as an archdiocese in 1958 — had been under apostolic administration; governed until 1982[17]

Benedito Calixto de Jesus Neto

Brazil

Architect of the new basilica, designed in the mid-1950s and under effective construction from 11 November 1955; grandson and namesake of the painter Benedito Calixto[14] [3]

Maria Helena Chartuni

Brazil

Head of the restoration department of the Museu de Arte de São Paulo (MASP); restored the shattered image between 28 June and 31 July 1978, under director Pietro Maria Bardi — a documented museum restoration, and the best-sourced individual act in this chronology[12]

Pe. Júlio J. Brustoloni, CSsR

Brazil

Redemptorist historian of the shrine; his 'História de Nossa Senhora da Conceição Aparecida' (1979) is the scholarly baseline for the 1717 dating — correcting the 1757 account's '1719, more or less' from the Count of Assumar's itinerary — and for the documentation of the fishermen in parish registers. He is nearly every secondary account's ultimate source, and consulting his book directly is this project's open task[9] [10]

Related organizations

Santuário Nacional de Nossa Senhora Aparecida

Founded First chapel blessed 1745; Basílica Velha inaugurated 1888; new basilica consecrated 1980; declared National Shrine by the CNBB in 1983 or 1984 (unresolved) · Aparecida, São Paulo, Brazil

The Redemptorist-run national shrine that keeps the image. It describes itself as 'the largest shrine in the world dedicated to Mary' — the Santuário's own claim, echoed by Church-adjacent sources, on a contested metric this dataset attributes and does not assert. Wikipédia-pt reports about 13 million visitors in the 2017 jubilee year.[3] [14]

Redemptorists (Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer, CSsR)

Founded 1732, Scala, Italy; at Aparecida since 28 October 1894 · Aparecida, São Paulo, Brazil

Custodians of the shrine since 28 October 1894, when missionaries of the Bavarian province arrived at the request of the Bishop of São Paulo; the São Paulo province continues the custody today, with a publishing and media apostolate that runs from the early Jornal Santuário to Rádio Aparecida, TV Aparecida and A12.com.[6] [8]

Archdiocese of Aparecida

Founded 19 April 1958, by Pius XII (apostolic constitution Sacrorum antistitum) · Aparecida, São Paulo, Brazil

The metropolitan see erected around the shrine — directly as an archdiocese in 1958, not a diocese later elevated; 1964 is the arrival of the first resident archbishop, Cardinal Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta, not an elevation. Its suffragans are Caraguatatuba, Lorena, São José dos Campos and Taubaté, and since November 2016 the shrine's basilica is its cathedral.[17] [15] [14]

A12.com / Rádio Aparecida / TV Aparecida

Founded Rádio Aparecida 1951; TV Aparecida 2005; A12.com as the shrine's web portal · Aparecida, São Paulo, Brazil

The shrine's Redemptorist media operation — 'A12' for the woman of Apocalypse 12. A12.com is the devotion's official web voice and the single largest source of institutional detail in this dataset; it is cited throughout as official-devotional, and dates resting on it alone are flagged as such.[8] [1]

CNBB — Conferência Nacional dos Bispos do Brasil

Founded 1952 · Brasília, Brazil

The Brazilian bishops' conference. The episcopate's collective devotion to Aparecida predates it — the 1901 coronation petition was the bishops' act half a century before the conference existed — and the CNBB continued the line: it declared the basilica the National Shrine (1983 or 1984; the primary act is unlocated), and its news service documents the 2017 golden rose. A conference honours and organizes a devotion; it issues no discernment judgment here, and none exists to issue.[18] [14] [4]

CELAM — Consejo Episcopal Latinoamericano

Founded 1955 · Bogotá, Colombia

The Latin American and Caribbean bishops' council, whose Fifth General Conference met at the shrine from 13 to 31 May 2007, inaugurated by Benedict XVI. Its conclusions — the 'Aparecida Document', whose drafting committee was chaired by the future Pope Francis — are a conference document on evangelization drafted at the shrine, not an act about the image; the cross-link exists because the document's name and the devotion's are constantly conflated.[21] [14]

Disambiguation & nuance

'Aparecida' does a lot of work in Portuguese and in Church documents — it can name an image, a devotion, a shrine, a see, a city and a conference document. The distinctions below are the ones most often flattened.

Not an apparition case — and the discernment Norms do not even apply

Nothing 'appeared' at Aparecida except a statue in a fisherman's net: 'Aparecida' names the image, not a vision, and in three centuries no apparition, locution or other phenomenon has been reported for any authority to judge. There is therefore no discernment judgment anywhere in this case, and none is missing. The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith's 'Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena' (17 May 2024) govern alleged supernatural phenomena — and the finding of a man-made image is not one, so the Norms have nothing to bite on here. Their art. 23, which states that as a rule neither bishops nor conferences nor the Dicastery declare such phenomena supernatural even in genuine cases, applies a fortiori: a chronology that reported a 'Church approval of the apparition of Aparecida' would be misreporting both this case and the Church's own rules.[24] [8]

Honours regulate worship; they authenticate nothing

The 1904 canonical coronation, the basilica titles of 1908 and 1980, the 1930 patronage decree, the golden roses of 1967, 2007 and 2017, and the consecrations of 1931 and 1980 are all acts about named objects — an image, a church, a nation's patronage. Each presupposes the devotion it honours and regulates the worship around it; none investigates or certifies the events of 1717. The Church grants the same acts to devotions with no reported event behind them at all — Our Lady of the Rosary and Our Lady of Mount Carmel have feasts, crowned images and basilicas, and there is nothing there to 'approve'. Reading Aparecida's honour ladder as an accumulating verdict gets the acts wrong one by one.[24] [8] [20]

The earliest account is from 1757, says '1719, more or less', and names no day

The devotion's own literature states the finding as 'October 1717' and the calendar says 12 October. What the record supports is narrower: the earliest surviving continuous account was written in August 1757 — forty years after the event — and opens 'No ano de 1719, pouco mais ou menos'; the year 1717 is a historians' inference from the Count of Assumar's documented itinerary; and no source attests a day, with the shrine saying 'second fortnight of October', one scholar's chain saying 17 October, and 12 October fixed by twentieth-century liturgical and civil acts, most visibly Law 6.802/1980. The date on the calendar is a convention honouring an event, not a record of it.[10] [9] [8]

Aparecida the city, the image, the shrine and the see

The town of Aparecida (SP) grew around the shrine on the Morro dos Coqueiros and was emancipated from Guaratinguetá in 1928; the Archdiocese of Aparecida dates from 1958; the National Shrine title from the 1980s. 'Aparecida' in a Church document may therefore denote the image, the devotion, the shrine building, the see or the city, and acts about one are routinely misread as acts about another — a basilica title is about a building, a patronage decree about a nation's devotion, an apostolic constitution about a see.[13] [15] [17]

The 'Aparecida Document' (2007) is CELAM's, not an act about the image

The 'Aparecida Document' is the concluding document of the Fifth General Conference of the Latin American and Caribbean bishops (CELAM), which met at the shrine from 13 to 31 May 2007. It is a programmatic text on evangelization — famous partly because the future Pope Francis chaired its drafting — that happens to be named for the place where it was written. It is not a judgment, honour or statement about the image or the events of 1717, and citations of 'Aparecida' in later magisterial texts are citations of the conference document.[21] [14]
